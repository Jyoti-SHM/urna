When we buy a home, we spend considerable time discussing what it costs. Then we spend the rest of our lives discovering what it truly costs.

We think about the EMIs. But then, there is electricity. Water. Hot water. Cooling. The lighting in the lobby. The pump in the basement. The lift that rises and descends every time you forget your specs in the car.

The power that keeps our private lives functioning, is invisible. But bloody expensive. The home is purchased just once. But the bills of living in it, continue to present themselves.

We have become remarkably blind to this bitter truth. We will negotiate fiercely over the price of a property, then move into a building designed to waste resources for the next 30 years.

Addressing tomorrow at Urna.

At Urna, we believe the latter needs to be addressed first. What will this home bill you after you own it? How much energy will it consume? How dependent will it be on water brought from elsewhere? What will it cost to keep comfortable? How far is the food from your table?

These are questions about ownership no one asks. But, you need to!

History offers an interesting lesson. The courtyard, the shaded verandah and the screened opening belonged to an architecture that understood its climate. Why do we not think of this for our apartments? Why do we instinctively think air-conditioning?

A comfortable room should benefit from the intelligence of its architecture as well as the capacity of its air-conditioner. Modern technology gives us more choices and many remotes. But it is not excuse to not think.

How can we lower tomorrow’s bills?

At Urna, efficient channeling of daylight reduces the need for artificial lighting. Thoughtful orientation, shading, vertical gardens, ventilation and material selection reduce heat entering the home. Efficient systems can lower the energy needed to provide comfort. Solar generation can meet part of the electricity demand, while water treatment and reuse can reduce the amount of freshwater the community needs to generate.

In an Urna home these ideas are brought together with the ambition of making luxury less demanding to maintain. We need that long shower, without guilt. Or a scary bill.

Of course, equipment costs money. Solar installations need servicing. Water-treatment systems need competent operation. Savings have to be assessed after these expenses, against actual consumption. A building cannot live on the enthusiasm and energy of its brochure.

But when the systems are well chosen and properly maintained, their benefits can recur for years. As you can see here, our calculations show how you start saving in an Urna home.

Most of us know the price of everything and the value of nothing, like a famous Oscar Wilde character, Lord Darlington. We can calculate the price of Italian marble to the last rupee while overlooking the value of a room that needs less cooling every summer.

The Urna difference.

Urna asks us to change our perspective. There is value in lower resource consumption. In expenses that can be avoided. In a balcony you use, daylight you enjoy and a building prepared to manage more of its own needs.

None of these demand that you become an ascetic. We insist that you retain the comfortable life. While the building works harder (and smarter) at providing it.

The Urna difference

The future will demand more from every home, more than our predecessors ever imagined or paid. A thoughtfully designed home begins preparing today for these demands. And for a smaller bill.
