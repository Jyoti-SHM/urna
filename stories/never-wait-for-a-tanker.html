In June 2026, residents of Evershine Cosmic in Andheri West were confronting an uncomfortable calculation. Their society needed two water tankers a day. Suppliers were demanding around ₹5,500 for a 20,000-litre load, up from ₹3,200–₹4,000. Even the higher price did not guarantee delivery.

At Jolly Maker 1 in Cuffe Parade, emergency measures included turning off the water overnight. A prestigious address could not persuade the tap to cooperate. These were among the experiences reported during Mumbai’s tanker strike by The Times of India

We ask whether a home has a sea view. And a pool. Perhaps we should also ask whether it has thought about water.

The future of water.

At Urna, the water question starts at the drawing board. Our vision is for residents to live without the recurring anxiety of scarcity. This means the building has to take responsibility for its water needs, before a shortage makes the decisions for us. See how we are designed for water-efficiency here.

The first task is to use less. Water-efficient taps, showers and flushing systems reduce unnecessary consumption. Their work is behind the wall, which explains why plumbing receives less attention than bath fittings, unfortunately. But only one of them will help when the water supply fails.

The second task is to help water work after its retirement. In a conventional building, freshwater performs a task and then, is immediately retired. The next task demands fresh water. In Urna homes, water is treated like a returning guest — it is welcomed, leaves, refreshes itself, returns.

Urna’s approach to water.

We never retire water. Used water has two separate lines, grey water and black water. Let’s look at both.

Water from showers is collected in the grey water line, filtered in an RO plant till the pH value goes back to 7.2. Then 50% of that goes back to domestic use and the rest is reused for car-washing, flushing etc. Your garden does not require drinking-quality water. Neither does the flush. Reserving freshwater for the uses that need it, allows the community’s supply to go further.

The black water that remains after all these processes is collected and sold to other construction sites. Separate plumbing keeps drinking water and treated water apart, allowing each to serve the purpose for which it is fit.

And then there is the water that falls from the sky. Rainwater harvesting collects runoff from rooftops and terraces for filtration, storage and appropriate use. Storage provides the buffer, treatment and reuse help stretch it. The water that runs off the land is channeled into recharge wells, which raises the water table in the entire area.

Smart water meters are installed so that you can track your water use and can be alerted in case of any leakage.

None of these systems works on noble thoughts alone. Treatment needs testing. Equipment needs maintenance. The arrangements must suit the apartment’s demand, available storage and replenishment.

But together, they change the building’s relationship with scarcity. It reduces demand, retains water and reuses it, rather than just place another call to a tanker. And be blackmailed.

There is a benefit beyond the compound too. A community that needs less freshwater places less pressure on the supply its neighbours share.

The Urna difference.

At Urna, we believe this is part of what a well-designed home owes its inhabitants and its city. Luxury should include the freedom to begin your morning without checking whether the tanker arrived. And the courage to flush without thinking twice.
