We ask a great many questions before buying a home. Which floor? How many bedrooms? What is the size of the pool? What is the view? How far is the school? Can the developer move that wall?

But we ask no questions about how the home will behave after we receive the OC. Because most of us do not know what questions to ask.

Will we have to turn on the lights during the day? Will the windows admit shaded daylight or merely provide an excellent view of the Jaypore curtains we have drawn against the glare? Will fresh air find a way through? Will our maintenance charge increase or decrease? Do we have water security? Where in the building are we using alternative energy?

The questions you need to ask.

A room’s comfort is decided before the furniture arrives. Its orientation, openings, shade and materials influence how much heat enters and how readily it escapes. Ventilation helps air move through occupied spaces. Thoughtfully admitted daylight makes a room useful and pleasant without requiring every switch to be on. These decisions play a role long after the architect has left.

Material choices become part of this daily relationship too. Materials selected to limit heat gain can help reduce the work required of cooling systems. The wall has a role beyond holding up the family photographs. Low-emission paints and finishes can reduce sources of indoor pollutants.

Urna and the elements.

At Urna, air, light, vegetation and space belong within the design brief. Our idea of design is not adding a few planters to the final rendering.

The balcony matters because somebody might sit there. The shared garden matters because somebody might walk through it, meet a neighbour or spend 10 incredibly important minutes doing nothing on Instagram.

Doing nothing, incidentally, deserves better architecture. We have become so accomplished at making every square foot account for itself that we sometimes forget to leave room for the person paying for it. A place to pause can be valuable without becoming a gym, a workstation or another amenity on a loaded brochure.

The Urna difference.

At Urna the guiding principles of Vayu and Akasha bring attention back to the elements — air that can move, space that can be inhabited with pleasure, a connection to the world beyond the glass. Check them out here.

We are not claiming that our buildings are physicians. Outdoor conditions, maintenance and the way you use you homes will continue to matter. But element-thoughtful architecture can create better conditions for everyday life instead of leaving every problem to a remote.

The most persuasive evidence may be quite ordinary. You read by daylight. You use the balcony. A shaded room stays comfortable for longer. You find yourself taking the longer route through the garden.

The Urna difference

Over time, the home’s habits become part of your own. At Urna, we believe this deserves more thought than the marble for the entrance lobby.
